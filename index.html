Executive Summary (Bloomington: What the labor market is doing to Cook)

Bloomington is a tight labor market with a specialized manufacturing cluster. The labor pool exists, but “available” labor is limited because unemployment averages ~3.6%. The standout signal is assembler concentration (LQ 3.97): Cook is operating in a market that is skills-aligned but highly contested.

Risk: hiring friction (low unemployment) Risk: wage pressure in hourly production Risk: forklift & “operators” are scarcity pockets Opportunity: internal ladders (assembler → inspection)
  • Primary constraint: labor tightness increases time-to-fill and raises the ROI of retention.
  • Primary battleground: $18–$22/hour roles (assemblers, movers, warehouse/forklift).
  • Potential bottleneck: maintenance (industrial machinery mechanics) — small base, high wages.

  • Warehousing + forklift demand is heavy (47 + 34 postings). Even if Cook is “manufacturing,” it competes with logistics employers for the same attendance/stability talent.

PESTLE Diagnosis (Non-Market Conditions — Bloomington)

Political / Legal

  • Indiana’s labor relations baseline (Right-to-Work) shapes expectations and bargaining environment.
  • Watch: incentives and development policies that accelerate competitor expansions (labor demand shocks).

Economic

  • Unemployment averaging ~3.56% indicates structurally tight labor conditions.
  • Watch: wage drift in $18–$22/hour roles and overtime dependency (early sign of shortage).

Social / Demographic

  • Institution-heavy market (IU + healthcare) shapes benefit expectations and job switching patterns.
  • Watch: labor force participation and “stable workforce” supply beyond student seasonality.

Technological

  • Opportunity: internal ladders (assembler → inspector/QA) and rapid training for entry roles.
  • Watch: competitor automation investments that shift demand toward tech/maintenance.

Environmental

  • Commute reliability affects hourly attendance; labor shed resilience matters for shift staffing.
  • Watch: winter disruption and commuting radius constraints.

Institutional / Ecosystem

  • Skills-aligned manufacturing cluster (high assembler/inspection concentration) supports hiring — but heightens competition.
  • Watch: large employers’ hiring waves (temp agencies and logistics employers are active competitors).

Recruitment & Retention Implications (Bloomington — actionable)

  • Compete on speed: in a tight market, reduce time-to-offer; consider same-day conditional offers for entry roles.
  • Win the $18–$22/hr band: align entry pay with assembler + forklift benchmarks; use shift premiums if 2nd/3rd shift is critical.
  • Protect bottlenecks: treat maintenance as a critical risk role (small base, high wage). Consider retention bonuses, training reimbursement, and clear progression.
  • Build ladders: formalize internal pathways (assembler → inspector/QA → lead/supervisor). The OEWS wage step-up supports the business case.
  • Acknowledge logistics competition: warehouse/forklift postings are abundant; messaging should emphasize stability, mission, and growth rather than “factory job.”

Shift note: Cook has not confirmed 1st vs 2nd/3rd shift targeting. Once confirmed, we will add a “shift differential pressure” panel (night/weekend pay patterns + competitor schedule signals).
