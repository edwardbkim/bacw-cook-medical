Executive Summary (What the market is doing to Cook)

Bloomington is a tight labor market with a manufacturing labor cluster. The labor pool exists (high assembler concentration), but “available” labor is limited (unemployment ~3.6%). The active postings snapshot (within 30 days) shows broad competition for production and logistics-adjacent workers, which raises the importance of offer competitiveness, speed-to-hire, and retention.

Risk: low unemployment → time-to-fill Risk: warehouse/forklift demand competes for same workforce Risk: maintenance & machining are bottleneck roles Opportunity: internal ladders (assembler → inspection/QA)
  • Primary battleground: $18–$22/hour roles (assemblers, movers, warehouse/forklift); this is where Cook wins or loses candidates
  • Structural advantage: high concentration of assembler/production talent locally (skills alignment)
  • Structural constraint: tight labor conditions + high employer competition reduce “ready supply”

PESTLE Diagnosis (Bloomington)

Political / Legal

  • Indiana’s labor relations baseline (Right-to-Work) shapes environment and expectations
  • Watch: incentives and development policies that accelerate competitor expansions (labor demand shocks)

Economic

  • Tight labor (~3.56% avg unemployment) increases vacancy cost; retention ROI is unusually high
  • Wage growth in assembler/supervisor benchmarks indicates rising expectations and compression risk

Social / Demographic

  • Bloomington is an institution-heavy market whcih shapes benefit expectations and job-switching patterns
  • Watch: labor force participation and “stable workforce” availability (beyond student seasonality)

Technological

  • Opportunity: internal ladders + rapid training for entry roles; move talent into inspection/QA and lead roles
  • Watch: competitor automation investments shifting demand toward maintenance/tech

Environmental

  • Commute reliability affects hourly attendance; labor shed resilience matters for shift staffing
  • Watch: seasonal disruption and commuting radius constraints

Institutional / Ecosystem

  • Skills-aligned manufacturing cluster supports hiring but heightens competition
  • Watch: temp agency activity and logistics hiring waves (major competitor channel for hourly labor)

Recruitment & Retention Recommendations (Bloomington)

  • Compete on speed: reduce time-to-offer for entry roles; consider same-day conditional offers and “fast-start” onboarding schedules
  • Win the $18–$22 band: align entry pay to assembler/forklift benchmarks; if hiring below band, offset with benefits and attendance incentives
  • Build a visible ladder: formalize internal pathways (assembler → inspector/QA → lead → supervisor) and communicate them at hiring
  • Protect bottlenecks: treat maintenance/machining as critical-risk roles (small base, high impact). Use retention bonuses, training reimbursement, and clear progression.
  • Address housing friction: consider shift premiums, transportation support, or retention bonuses that acknowledge the rent affordability threshold
  • Shift follow-up: once Cook confirms 1st vs 2nd/3rd shift needs, add a “shift differential pressure” panel (market shift premiums + competitor schedules)
